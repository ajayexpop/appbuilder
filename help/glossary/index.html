Glossary

162 words used in App Studio and in this guide, each defined from App Studio itself.

A

A row that is missing
Each part of a tile is read on its own, and a part that cannot be read is left out rather than guessed: then there are no store rows, no build row or no event row. A missing “Latest test build” row can also mean the app has no test build yet.
Accent colour
A mark for words in a Play & Win text. The app draws them in the event's accent colour; App Studio's editor shows them in its own brand colour.
Play & Win
Account type (screenshots)
Every screenshot run takes one pass as a visitor and one as an exhibitor. The gallery groups pictures by account type.
Store screenshots
AI assistant preparation
Before the assistant can answer, the event is prepared: its content is sent to the assistant. Saving the home with the AI Synapsium block switched on, or with AI assistant as the Button in the top right, starts it. People see no assistant button until it is Ready. The briefing, what only you know, what it may read and the limits are never sent to phones.
Home screen
Analytics service
Where the Analytics dashboard's activity numbers come from: counts over daily summaries of what people did in the app. The figures about installed phones come from the install records instead.
Analytics
App Bundle (AAB) and APK
The two Android file formats: the App Bundle is “What Google Play and its testing need. It cannot be installed on a phone by hand.”; the APK “Installs straight on an Android phone and builds faster. It cannot go to Google Play. If the app has its own store key, the App Bundle is made as well.”
Build & test
App number
The number in the grey badge under the app's name, as in “App 231”. The search matches it, and an app with no name is called “App [number]”.
App Studio session
Opening App Studio from your admin starts a session of up to 8 hours. Opening it again from your admin starts a fresh one.
App-wide
Everything on this page is one setting for the whole app: not per event and not per language. The name is one text for every language.
Name, icon & splash
Approved
The release passed review: the state that follows “In review” and comes before the rollout. Your ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout; ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
At least 3 people (search terms)
A search term is listed only when at least 3 different people searched for it.
Analytics
At least 5 measurements (rankings)
On Performance, a screen, server call, app version or phone needs at least 5 measurements to appear in a ranking. Screen and server-call times are measured on a random 1 in 10.
Analytics

B

Block
Text, or one of the Home screen's blocks with the same settings. A page offers every Home screen body block except the AI Synapsium block and the Original home page.
Custom pages
Bottom bar
Up to four items of the event's whole menu can be tabs in the app's bottom bar, beside the menu button. Hidden items still count. The tabs follow the order in which their switches were turned on, not the order of the sections.
Menu & content
Build
One run that makes the app's installable file for one platform, iPhone (iOS) or Android, always from the code ExpoPlatform deploys. It is queued, goes through its stages and ends “Ready” or “Failed”. An iOS and an Android build can run at the same time; a platform's build button is greyed out while a build of that platform runs.
Build & test
Build number
Each build gets the next number of the app's own series, given out for good when the build is queued; the card shows “[version] (build [number])”.
Build & test
Build pictures
App Studio treats the icon and the splash picture as pictures that go into the app build: they are kept at the size they were framed, while other pictures are capped at 2048 px on their longest side.
Name, icon & splash
Build status
Queued, Building, Ready (the build succeeded) or Failed.
Built-in screen
A screen the app already has, such as Exhibitors or the Floor plan. Custom pages lists where each sits and can add one to a menu section.
Custom pages
Bundle id
The app's id: lower-case words joined by dots, at least three (com.example.app). App Studio gives every new app one automatically, made from your ExpoPlatform admin's address and the app's number. You see it only if that id is already used by another app; then you type another.

C

Capture (screenshot run)
One run takes the screenshots of one event for one store. It waits for its app build if it had to start one (“Building the app”), queues (“Waiting to start”), runs on a test device (“Capturing”) and ends “Ready” or “Failed”. A run that sends no news for 60 minutes is marked failed the next time the page reads the runs. The list shows the newest runs first, at most 20.
Store screenshots
Change list
“What changed since the previous build”: the app changes between this build and the previous finished build of the same platform. It is written once per pair of builds, from the code changes, by an AI model, and stored; it can include lines for the store's “What's new”.
Build & test
Comparison with the previous period
The line under a headline tile: “Previous period: 1,204”, the change in percent, and an arrow. The same figure over the same number of days just before the chosen period. The percentage is the change against that earlier value. Green is better, red is worse. On Performance lower is better for every tile: the times and “Server calls failed”. “Nothing to compare in the previous period” means the earlier value was zero.
Analytics
Composition system
One of six layouts for the whole set: Runway, Stand, Spectrum, Editorial, Frame and Immersive. Only Runway and Spectrum draw a phone body; the others show the capture without one.
Store screenshots
Contrast check
App Studio measures the Tertiary colour on the Primary colour and warns below 4.5:1, offering a darker Primary. It is advice only; saving is not blocked.
Colours & fonts
Copied once from the old App Builder
The first time anyone opens Events & updates, Sign-in & registration or Menu & content for your app, App Studio starts from your old App Builder's settings, in every language, and keeps them as its own copy: the app's settings, and the sign-in settings and menu of every event. From then on App Studio shows and saves that copy: the old App Builder is not read again for these settings, and nothing you save here changes your old apps. The one setting also written to your admin is the choice of events, because your admin serves the app's list of events.
Events & updates
Counter
One number on Event stats: Upcoming Meetings, Upcoming Sessions, Scanned Badges, Chats or Unread Messages. The widget shows up to three; the first three are the default. Each person sees their own numbers.
Home-screen widgets
Counts of actions
Most figures named after an action - views, opens, requests, scans - count how many times it happened, not how many people did it. Each record the app sent is one. When a phone gets no answer after sending its records, it sends them again; distinct counts (people, devices) are not affected, and action counts very rarely count such a resend twice.
Analytics
Custom pages and the Home screen
Different sections. Home screen sets the app's home; a custom page is a separate page, reached from the menu or from a link (buttons, tiles, pictures, text links and HTML links can open “page:[id]”). Pages use the same kinds of blocks as the home.
Custom pages

D

Default
An empty field means nothing is set in App Studio, so the app uses its current value. The page shows it as “Default: …”, in the select's first item “Default (…)”, or as the current picture. Saving a field empty goes back to the old App Builder's value.
Name, icon & splash
Default and your value
An empty field means nothing is set here, so the app keeps using its current value; the field shows it as “Default: [value]”. Once you set a value, “Use default ([value])” clears it again.
Colours & fonts
Default text and per-language text
An empty text uses the default shown under it. Header texts have a value per language (EN, DE, FR, ES, IT, RU); block texts follow the event's languages.
Home screen
Demo account
The login the store reviewers use: a login, a password (never shown again once saved), the demo event number and optional notes. Each iPhone store submission files it in App Store Connect's review details, with ExpoPlatform Support as the review contact; without it the iPhone submission fails and the release stays “Ready”.
Publish
Designed home screen
One switch for the whole app. On, the app opens on the designed home; off, it opens on its first menu screen and the design is not used. It is off for a new app unless the app was copied from one where it is on.
Home screen
Distinct count
People, signed-in accounts and devices are counted once each over the whole period, not added up day by day. These counts are estimates (accurate to within about 1-2% on large numbers; small numbers exact). Some cards count devices exactly: New vs returning and Who comes back; Languages, Where people are and Top cities on Audience, and Operating systems and Phones on Tech, which count the phones' own check-ins; and every number on the Devices tab.
Analytics
Draft
“It starts as a draft: nothing reaches the stores until you build and publish it.” Creating an app starts no build and no store submission; building is done in Build & test and publishing in Publish. Before the first build, ExpoPlatform's team sets the app up in the App Store and Google Play.
Draft and two saves
Home screen edits stay in a draft until “Save home screen”. Colours & fonts has its own “Save changes”. Pictures and videos are stored as soon as they are uploaded, and “Restore the default” saves at once.
Home screen
Draft on Google Play (staged)
When the app's Android listing is set to stage releases, a send to Google Play internal testing waits as a draft (“Staged on Play”) until it is published in Google Play Console.
Build & test
Draft, 'Save changes' and uploads
Edits made on the page stay there until “Save changes”. The item editor's “Save” saves the whole menu at once, with every edit then on the page. Switching the event or the language drops unsaved edits without asking. Uploads do not wait: a document, picture or own icon is stored as soon as it is uploaded, even if the menu is never saved.
Menu & content

E

Entry
One guess with the person's first and last name, e-mail, the company typed on the kiosk, the source (App or Kiosk), when it was sent, when consent was given and the consent version. The list shows every entry of the event, whatever the attendee's Networking setting.
Play & Win
Event dates
Shown as day, short month and year (en-GB) in the event's own time zone, or UTC when it is unknown; a range shows “[start] – [end]”.
Event page
A page made before App Studio. It is listed here but edited in Menu & content; App Studio offers no delete for it here.
Custom pages
ExpoPlatform's approval
A setting for each app, on by default: your publication request waits for your ExpoPlatform team to approve or decline it. When they switch it off for your app, “Submit” publishes at once and the toast reads “Sent to the stores”.
Publish

F

Fallback to English
English is the base language. When a language has no text for a key, the app shows the English text for that key, key by key, unless you set your own wording in that language. In the editor such a row reads “No standard text”.
Translations
Feature video
A clip ExpoPlatform records on Android for a Google Play declaration: Google asks for a video because the app keeps recording meeting notes in the background. In App Studio it can be watched and downloaded, not recorded or deleted.
Store screenshots
Featured event
The one event a tile shows: the event on now (“Happening now”), else the next one to start (“Next event”), else the one that ended last (“Last event”). Events without dates are never featured. When the app has more than one event, the label adds the total, as in “Next event · 3 events”.
Features you can add
The features offered in “Add to this section” come from the event: a feature whose module is off for the event is not offered, and one already in the section is not offered again.
Menu & content
Filters
Event, Period, Platform and User type narrow every number on the tab. Period: whole days in UTC, up to 92 days, today included; the presets end today. Event: only what was done while that event was chosen in the app. Platform: iOS or Android. User type: the kind of account the person was signed in with. Older app versions do not report the user type; their activity drops out as soon as a user type is chosen, and shows as “Unknown” under Overview > User types. A few cards read the phones' own check-ins instead of the activity records and ignore User type: the map, Top cities, Languages, Operating systems and Phones. The event picker's people counts come from the activity records but ignore Platform and User type. The filters are kept in App Studio's own page address, so a reload of your admin's page in the same browser tab reopens the same tab with the same filters.
Analytics
First visit
The first time a client opens a page in this browser; that page's part then opens by itself.
Fixed rows
Rows the app manages. “Sync progress banner” and “Pending changes banner” are pinned at the top and cannot be moved, switched or removed. “Log in prompt” and “See all events” (for guests) can be moved and switched off, but not removed.
Home screen

G

Google Play internal testing
Google Play's internal test track for the Android app. Testers are Google Groups; “Everyone in a group can test.” On screen: “Ready for your testers within minutes, with no review.”
Build & test
Guide
App Studio's built-in guide. For the page on screen it dims the page, lights one part at a time and explains it.

H

Home screen blocks versus Home-screen widgets
Home screen blocks (this page) are the parts of the app's own first screen: agenda, counters, buttons, pictures and so on, set per event and saved with “Save home screen”. Home-screen widgets (Features > Home-screen widgets) are the widgets people put on their phone's home screen, outside the app: Event stats, My agenda and Ask the assistant, each switched on or off there. The “Ask the assistant” widget takes its title, line and assistant name from the AI Synapsium block here.
Home screen
Home-screen widget
A small panel of your app that people place on their phone's own home screen, outside the app. App Studio offers three: Event stats, My agenda and Ask the assistant. The settings are per app, not per event or language.
Home-screen widgets

I

In-app address
An address that opens a screen (menu:[key]) or a page (page:[id]) inside the app; listed under “Links for your own HTML” and accepted in links.
Custom pages
Inside your admin only
App Studio opens only from your own ExpoPlatform admin. A link or bookmark to App Studio's own address does not sign you in.
Install link
A web page from which testers install a build; when a build has one, its card shows it with a QR code.
Build & test
Install records
App Studio's record of each installed copy of the app and its latest check-in: platform, app version and build, system version, phone brand and model, language, the person signed in, and the city and country located from the phone's network. It feeds Languages, the map, Top countries and Top cities (Audience), Operating systems and Phones (Tech), phone names on Performance, and the whole Devices tab. Figures from it follow the event, the period and the platform, but not the user type.
Analytics

J

Jump-to name
An optional name on a block (lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, unique on the page) that a link can jump to, as the hint says.
Custom pages

K

Key
The name of a text in the app, shown in small type under each standard text (for example about.copyright). The list is sorted by key, and the search also matches keys.
Translations
Kiosk
A public web page for a stand iPad, reached only through its private link; no sign-in. Keep the link private: anyone who has it can enter the draw. The kiosk shows the voting texts, the consent, the thank-you screen and the closed screen; it has no rules sheet and no final screen.
Play & Win

L

Language of the texts
The language whose texts you are editing. Switching it reloads the section and drops unsaved changes without a warning. In Events & updates, the choice of events is one for the whole app, in every language.
Events & updates
Language you are writing in
The one language switch of a page (EN, DE, FR, ES, IT, RU). English is required for the title and for every Text block; the editor says English shows wherever a language is left empty.
Custom pages
Languages and missing texts
The app's built-in texts come in 22 languages: English (2,672 texts) and ar, de, es, es-419, fi, fr, is, it, ja, kk, ko, nl, pl, pt, ru, sv, th, tr, vi, zh and zh-TW. Every language other than English lacks 2 of the English texts; ja, ko, th, vi, zh and zh-TW lack 47. The app shows those in English unless you set your own wording. The Language list offers only the languages ExpoPlatform has switched on, by English name and code, for example “German (de)”.
Translations
Languages of a run
A run from App Studio takes one pass per language of the app's store listing, or English when there is no listing language yet.
Store screenshots
Latest activity
When the newest record inside the chosen period and filters was made - “Latest activity 3 min ago”. The time of the newest action recorded under the current filters. It is worked out when the numbers are read and does not move on its own. Phones send their records every minute or every 50 actions, and a phone without a connection keeps them for up to 30 days and sends them when it is back online. Numbers are usually complete within a few minutes; a period that just ended can still grow a little as offline phones catch up. “Refresh” reads the open tab again. Another tab is not read again when you open it: it keeps the numbers it read for the same filters until you press “Refresh” there.
Analytics
Latest test build
The app's newest test build on either platform: “Building” (queued or building), “Ready” (finished) or “Failed”, then “iOS” or “Android”, its version, and how long ago it finished (or started, or was queued).
Light and dark colours
Each event has a light colour set and a separate dark set. In dark mode a colour left empty uses its light one. Fonts, the sponsor bar colour and the pictures are one set for both.
Colours & fonts
Light or dark, and Brand colour
Light or dark follows the phone's light or dark mode; the preview then shows a Light and a Dark card. Brand colour gives the widget your brand colour; the preview shows one Brand colour card, painted with the first event's Primary colour from Colours & fonts, or with the previews' own default colour when none is set.
Home-screen widgets
Listing slot
Where an attached size goes on the store listing: “iPhone screenshots” (iPhone 6.9 inch, 1 to 10), “iPad screenshots” (iPad 13 inch, 1 to 10), “Phone screenshots” (Android phone, 2 to 8) and “10-inch tablet screenshots” (Android 10 inch tablet, 2 to 8). iPhone 6.5 inch has no slot and goes to the download only.
Store screenshots
Live in the stores (Overview)
On the Overview, a store counts as live only when App Studio's own store record is marked live. Your apps also counts the version each store reports as live; the Overview does not.
Live phone preview
The real mobile app running inside the page. It uses your app's settings, menu and theme plus the edits you have not saved yet; everything else (content, people) is sample data. Any sign-in works, and nothing done inside it is saved or sent; only pictures are loaded.
Live preview
The phone beside the form runs the real app in the browser, with the unsaved changes and sample content (“Live app · real config & menu · mock content · tap to navigate”). “Restart app (replay splash)” starts it again from the splash.
Name, icon & splash

M

Must update / Asked to update
Per platform, the version below which people must update before they can use the app (“Must update”), and the version below which they are asked to update and may skip it (“Asked to update”). “Off” means that kind of update is not asked. App Studio's own wording: “Older versions are asked, or made, to update when the app starts.” Set by your ExpoPlatform team.

N

Names and ids
Exhibitors, sessions, documents, sponsors and campaigns are shown by name with their id below it. Names come from the event's own catalogue. With “All events”, the catalogues of the app's busiest events are used. Something no catalogue knows any more - deleted, or from an event no longer in the app - shows its id alone.
Analytics
Newest build
Builds are listed newest first. The app card shows the newest iOS build and the newest Android build. The build step looks at the newest build of either platform and at the newest build that succeeded.
Next build versus next refresh
Switching a widget on or off takes effect with your next build. What a widget shows updates on phones at their next refresh, with no new build.
Home-screen widgets
No App Studio password
App Studio has no sign-in form of its own. Your ExpoPlatform admin account is your App Studio account: you always open App Studio from your admin.
Not the same as the Home screen's blocks
These phone widgets are not the blocks of the app's own Home screen, which are set in the Home screen section. The Home screen has, for example, its own stats block with its own list of numbers. Only Ask the assistant borrows from the Home screen: its title, its line and the assistant's name come from the AI Synapsium block there.
Home-screen widgets
Not the same as the phone preview
The phone preview beside the page always shows Play & Win with the app's default texts and voting open, whatever is set here, so a Play & Win menu row can be tried in the preview.
Play & Win

O

Older deployment
App Studio was updated while your page was open. Saving from that page does nothing until you reload it.
One draft with Name, icon & splash
Colours & fonts and Name, icon & splash are one form with one draft and one “Save changes”. Switching between the two keeps unsaved edits, and saving either page saves both.
Colours & fonts
One form with Colours & fonts
Name, icon & splash and Colours & fonts are one form with one draft and one “Save changes”. Moving between the two keeps unsaved edits, and saving on either page saves both.
Name, icon & splash
One home per event
Each event has its own home. An event with none of its own shows the app-wide home, else the built-in default. An app without events edits the app-wide home directly. The AI Synapsium block can only sit on an event's home.
Home screen
One menu per event
Each event has one menu. Menu & content and your ExpoPlatform team edit the same menu, so a change saved by either shows in the other and reaches the app. A page you add to the menu from Custom pages joins it, listed as “Your page”.
Menu & content
One shared form
Name, icon & splash, Colours & fonts, Home screen, Custom pages and Play & Win are parts of one form with one preview, so moving between them keeps unsaved edits.
Original home page: linked or copied
Linked: one copy, edited where it always was; the home shows it and it leaves the menu. Copied: a Custom HTML block holding the page in every language, yours to edit; later changes to the original do not reach it.
Home screen
Over the air (no new build)
Saved texts reach installed apps without a new build. The app checks for changed texts when it opens, when it comes back to the foreground and every 5 minutes while open, at most once every 30 minutes, and applies a changed language at once. On start it uses the texts it downloaded last, so it works offline.
Translations

P

Palette
Aurora, Midnight and Editorial are ready-made sets of all 21 event colours. Pressing one fills the fields once; nothing links the event to the palette afterwards.
Colours & fonts
Part of the Guide (chapter)
The Guide's steps for one page: Your apps, or one section of an app. The Guide itself says “Each page has its own part.”; the last step of a part offers the next one.
People, signed-in people and devices
Three different ways of counting who used the app. A person is a signed-in account; before someone signs in, the phone stands for them. “Signed in” counts only accounts. “Devices” counts installed copies of the app, whoever uses them. Someone who used the app before signing in, then signed in during the same period, can count twice under People: once as the phone, once as the account. One person with the app on two phones is one signed-in person and two devices. Distinct counts are estimates accurate to within about 1-2% on large numbers; small numbers are exact. Per-day figures are each their own count: the days of a chart do not add up to the period's total, because someone active on three days is counted on each.
Analytics
Per event
Colours, fonts, pictures and the missing-picture choice belong to one event. Only the event picker palette is for the whole app, and only apps with several events have it.
Colours & fonts
Per event, and per language for texts
The menu belongs to the event chosen at the top. Item names, section headers and page content (tabs, documents, contacts, social networks) are kept per language. Everything else (which items, their order, icons, bottom bar, “Visible for”, “Hide this item”, guests only, web page address, document password) is one setting for all languages. A name or section header never written in a language shows the English one, else the first one written, and page content shows the English one. A section header you empty and save stays empty.
Menu & content
Per event, per language
Each event has its own sign-in and registration settings. Only the texts (the sign-in, web sign-in, scan and register button texts) are kept per language; everything else holds one value for every language, so changing it under one language changes it for all. “Native registration form” and “AI meeting notes” are for the whole app.
Sign-in & registration
Period (UTC days)
Whole days in UTC, from and to both included, at most 92 days, never past today; the default is the last 30 days. The previous period is the same number of days just before it.
Analytics
Plain count
Counts of actions (views, opens, requests, scans) add up the records the phones sent; a batch the phone sent twice after a lost answer can very rarely count twice.
Analytics
Play & Win
A guessing game run per event: attendees guess a number, agree to a separate consent, and you draw the prize from the entries. Each event has its own settings and its own entries. One entry per attendee per event; one entry per e-mail address per event across the app and the kiosk.
Play & Win
Play & Win consent and its version
A consent of its own, separate from the app's other consents, that an attendee ticks before the vote is sent. Its version is a short code worked out from the wording itself, so any change in any language makes a new version; each entry records the version its attendee agreed to.
Play & Win
Preview
A drawing of the phone widget with sample content (numbers, an agenda, words), painted in the widget's own colours rather than App Studio's. While a widget is off, its cards read “Not enabled for this event”.
Home-screen widgets
Publication request
What “Submit to the stores” sends: one finished, signed build per store, one request at a time. It is waiting, approved, declined (with ExpoPlatform's reason) or failed (approved, but publishing could not start). On approval, ExpoPlatform's system sends the listing to the stores and creates and submits one store release per build.
Publish
Published under
The store account the app is published from, chosen by ExpoPlatform when it sets the app up. App Studio does not show the account's name: it reads “Our store account”.
Publish

R

Release journey
Six phases per store, worked out from the app's data: Set up (every set-up step that holds the release is done), Build (a signed build for this store exists), Test (the newest test release is live for testers), Store listing (the review-readiness check is clear and, for the app's first release, this store's listing is complete and in the store), Publish (the newest store release is live) and Live (a version is live in the store). Only fully done phases count in “[n] of 6 steps done”; the first phase not done is the current one.
Publish
Release on its way (second badge)
Shown when the app's newest production release for that store is in one of these states, followed by its version: “Preparing” (draft, uploading, processing, ready or staged), “In review” (submitted or in review), “Approved”, “Rolling out”, “Rollout stopped” (halted) or “Rejected”. Releases on other channels, and other states, add no badge.
Reloading your admin's page
Reloading your ExpoPlatform admin's App Studio page opens App Studio again. It then reopens the page of your app that this browser tab showed, instead of “Your apps”. After you press “Your apps”, or in a new browser tab, it opens on the list; so it does when the browser keeps no site data.
Review-readiness check
A check that runs when Publish opens: is a demo account saved, can it sign in to the demo event, does that event have at least 3 sessions on a non-empty agenda, and, for Google Play, has the data safety form been sent. The verdict is kept for 5 minutes; “Check again” runs it afresh. Items marked “Your move” are yours; “Our team” items are ExpoPlatform's. The same verdict feeds the Overview's “What now” box and the Store review block.
Publish
Rollout
How a store-approved release reaches people step by step. An app's first-ever iPhone version is released by Apple on approval; on Android the release is sent at a first stage of 5%. Starting the rollout beyond that (later iPhone versions, Android past 5%) is done by ExpoPlatform; while it runs, health checks (crash-free sessions, and app-not-responding on Android) decide whether it moves forward, holds or stops. Your ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout; ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
Publish

S

Screenshot build
A build of the app made for capturing: instead of a sign-in it opens on a choice of account type, and its people are sample people. “Take screenshots” reuses the newest screenshot build of the app for that store; when there is none it starts a new app build first.
Store screenshots
Search terms, filters and settings
Search terms are cut to 64 characters, and a term only appears once at least 3 different people searched for it. Filters, settings and profile edits record which ones were used, not their values.
Analytics
Sections and groups
App Studio splits an app's settings into sections, listed on the left in a fixed order: Overview, then the groups App, Look, Features, Release and After launch.
Send to testers (a test release)
A release of one store-signed build on TestFlight or Google Play internal testing, created and submitted by “Send”. An app takes one release at a time per platform, store releases included: while one is still moving, another send on that platform is not possible.
Build & test
Sessions
One stretch of using the app. A session starts when the app is opened and ends after 30 minutes without any activity. Its length is the time from its first to its last recorded action, so a session with a single action lasts 0 minutes. A session belongs to the day and period it started in.
Analytics
Set-up steps
The store set-up checklist of each store app (12 steps for an iPhone app, 13 for Android), done by ExpoPlatform in App Store Connect and the Google Play Console. The only step that is yours is “Demo account for reviewers”, which saving the demo account marks. Steps marked “Optional” do not hold the release back.
Publish
Settings from the old App Builder
Each event's sign-in and registration settings start from the old App Builder's values, in every language: App Studio copies every event the first time anyone opens Events & updates, Sign-in & registration or Menu & content for the app. From then on App Studio keeps its own copy: what you save here is what your app uses for that event, and later changes in the old App Builder do not reach it. Nothing saved here reaches the old apps. An event not copied yet (its copy failed, or it was added since) keeps what your ExpoPlatform admin sends until the next open copies it.
Sign-in & registration
Several events / One event
Several events: “The app offers every event with the tags you choose, and new ones as you tag them.” One event: “The app opens straight into one event.” The events and their tags come from your ExpoPlatform admin.
Several events, by event tag / One event
The two ways to choose the events your app offers: by the tags you give events in your admin, or one single event. The events and their tags come from your admin.
Events & updates
Shares, tables and empty cards
How percentages, long tables and cards without data are shown. Percentages have one decimal; “—” means there was nothing to divide by. Tables show their first 8 rows with “Show all” for the rest. A card with nothing in the period says so in its own words; a whole tab with nothing offers to clear the filters or widen the period to 90 days.
Analytics
Sign-in modes
“In the app”: people sign in with the app's own form. “On a web page”: they sign in on a web page, the “Web sign-in address” or the event's own. “Both”: the app offers both, with a second button labelled by “Web sign-in button”.
Sign-in & registration
Sign-in pictures, colours and fonts
Each event also keeps sign-in pictures (logo, list background, loading screen), button colours and fonts from the old App Builder. The app receives them and the preview draws the logo, list background and button colours, but this section has no field to change them.
Sign-in & registration
Single and repeatable blocks
Button row, Tile grid, Image row, Section heading, Custom HTML and Hero banner can be added more than once. Every other block can be on the home once, and leaves the tray when it is placed.
Home screen
Skipped step
A step whose element is not on the page is skipped by itself: for example “Create an app” without a “New app” button, “First, ExpoPlatform sets the app up” once building is possible, or “Before the stores” while the set-up steps list is closed.
Standard text
The text the app shows in a language when you have set no wording of your own: the app's built-in text, or ExpoPlatform's shared change to it. You see only the result, not which part comes from where. The editor shows it at the left of each row and greyed inside the empty field.
Translations
Start from (copy)
A new app starts either on “A new design” (the design ExpoPlatform set for new App Studio apps) or as a copy of one of your apps. A copy takes that app's saved design (app-wide and per event), its Designed home screen switch and its event stats, agenda and assistant widget settings. Its registration mode setting is not copied, and the name and events are the ones given in the dialog.
Step
One popup of a part. It points at an element of the page, or, without one, shows in the middle of the screen.
Step states
Each step in “Next steps” is done (green tick), to do (grey circle), in progress (clock, brand colour) or a problem (warning sign). Only done steps count in “[done] of [n] done.”.
Store app
The app's record in one store: an App Store app and a Google Play app. ExpoPlatform creates them when it sets the app up in the stores; until then Publish shows “Not in the stores yet”. Each store app has a state (Not in the store yet, In the store, not live, Live, Suspended, Removed) and an App ID (bundle id).
Publish
Store checks
Lines that read the set against Apple's and Google's rules: “to fix” lines block the attach and the download until fixed; “to consider” lines are warnings.
Store screenshots
Store listing
The app's page in the stores: one listing for both stores, with texts, links and pictures per language. Its status is “Not sent yet”, “Changes not sent”, “Sending to the stores”, “In the stores” or “Sending failed”. Saved texts and pictures stay in App Studio until ExpoPlatform sends the listing to the stores, which happens when they approve a publication request.
Publish
Store release
One build on its way to a store's production track. Its steps are Draft, Uploading, Processing, Ready, Submitted, In review, Approved, Rolling out and Live; it can also be Halted, or end Rejected, Failed, Cancelled or Superseded. Ready releases are sent for review automatically. After that, the store's own reports move the release on: to In review, then to Approved or Rejected.
Publish
Store set-up
Before an app made in App Studio can be built, ExpoPlatform sets it up in the App Store and Google Play under a publishing account it chooses. Until then, or if it declined, building is held and the page says why.
Build & test
Store setup
Creating an app sends ExpoPlatform a request to set it up in the App Store and Google Play; ExpoPlatform's team chooses the store account it is published under. Until the request is approved, the app cannot be built, and Build & test says “ExpoPlatform is setting this app up in the App Store and Google Play - you can build it once that is done”. If the team declines it, Build & test says “ExpoPlatform did not set this app up in the stores”, followed by their reason when they give one.
Store status (first badge)
What people can download now from that store. “Live · [version]”: the store itself reports that version as live (“Live” alone when no version name is known). “Not live yet”: the app has a store record for that store, but nothing is live. “Not published”: the app has no store record for that store. “Removed from the store”: the store record is suspended or removed.
Store studio set
The set you compose in Store studio: the composition, captions, frame, screen order and the other settings. It is held in the page and never saved; leaving or reloading the page loses it. Two actions do reach App Studio: “Use this” uploads a framed picture to your screenshots, and “Attach to the store listing” replaces one listing language's pictures. Only screens “In the set” that have a picture are written.
Store screenshots
Store submission price (App Store)
Each iPhone store submission sets the app's App Store price schedule to the free price point.
Publish
Store-signed
A finished build signed for the store. Only store-signed builds can be sent to testers; an unsigned build cannot be sent.
Build & test
Stored at once, used after Save
A picture or video is stored for the app as soon as it is uploaded or fetched from a link. The app uses it only after “Save changes”.
Name, icon & splash
Switch versus Save changes
A widget's On/Off switch saves at once, using the widget's last saved counters and colours. Counters and colours are only staged until you click Save changes, which saves all three widgets in one go.
Home-screen widgets

T

Tab
A part of a page with its own name, blocks, spacing and background. With more than one tab, the page shows them as a strip along its top (as the editor says).
Custom pages
Test device
Google Play runs use an Android emulator, first with a Pixel 7 phone profile and then with a Nexus 10 tablet profile, with the status bar set to 09:41, a full battery, full Wi-Fi and no notifications. App Store runs use an iPhone simulator of the 6.9-inch class (iPhone 17 Pro Max or iPhone 16 Pro Max) and an iPad Pro 13-inch simulator, and the run checks that every picture is a size the App Store accepts.
Store screenshots
Testers' personal data
The TestFlight list shows each tester's email address and, when App Store Connect has it, first and last name; Google Play shows Google Group addresses only. Addresses you add are sent to Apple or Google.
Build & test
TestFlight
Apple's test channel for the iPhone app. Testers are people invited by email into the app's TestFlight test group (at most 100 in an internal group, 10,000 in an external one). On screen: “Apple checks the build first, usually within a day. Then your testers get an email invite.”
Build & test
The app's own wording (default texts)
Every Play & Win text has a wording that ships inside the app for each of its 22 languages. A text you leave alone shows that wording; if a language has none, App Studio shows the English one. Your own text replaces it in one language only.
Play & Win
The four menu sections
“Event (everyone)”, “My account (participants)”, “My company (team members)” and “My company (exhibitor)”. In the app, Event is shown to everyone, including people who are not signed in; My account to signed-in people except exhibitor company owners; My company (team members) to signed-in team members; My company (exhibitor) to signed-in exhibitor company owners. People who are not signed in see only Event items.
Menu & content
The sign-in preview
The phone beside the form runs the real app, signed out, on the chosen event, with the unsaved settings applied: sign-in mode and address, the button texts, the event-name switch, the register button and where it leads. It also draws the event's stored logo, list background and sign-in button colours (an empty picture keeps the event's own). It always opens on the sign-in screen, whichever “When people must sign in” is chosen. It shows only on wide screens. There is no “View as” choice here; that belongs to the Menu & content preview.
Sign-in & registration
Typical and slowest 5%
The two ways every time on Performance is given. “Typical” is the median: half of the measurements were faster. “Slowest 5%” means all but the slowest one in twenty were faster. Both are accurate to about 1%. Screen and server-call times are measured on a random 1 in 10 of them, to spare people's phones; their “Samples” and “Calls” columns count those measurements, not all screens or calls. A screen, server call, version or phone needs at least 5 measurements to appear in a ranking.
Analytics

U

Unsaved changes
Edits stay in the form, and in the preview, until “Save changes”. Switching the event or the language drops them without asking.
Sign-in & registration
Unsaved edits per event
Edits are kept per event in the browser until you save or discard them; Save Play & Win saves the selected event only.
Play & Win
Unsaved page
What you write is kept only in this browser tab until “Save page”: one unsaved page per event. “All pages” keeps it (the list offers “Return to it”); closing the browser tab loses it, and the browser asks before leaving while there are unsaved changes.
Custom pages
Update prompts
Set by your ExpoPlatform team. They ask people who have an older version of the app to update it. You cannot change them in App Studio; the Overview shows them.
Events & updates
Uploads are stored at once
A picture is stored for the app as soon as it is uploaded or fetched from a link, before Save. The app uses it only after “Save changes”.
Colours & fonts

V

Version live in the store (App updates)
The newest version of your app that was released through App Studio's system to that store and is fully live there. A version still rolling out step by step, or halted, does not count. A version published another way is not counted either, so the line can read “Not live yet” for an app that is in the store.
Visible for
Who sees an item in the app's menu. Nothing ticked means everyone who can see the section; anything ticked limits it to those people and hides it from people who are not signed in.
Menu & content
Voting window
Voting is open during the event's own dates, or before them while Test mode is on; it is never open once the campaign is finished. An attendee who did not vote sees the Voting closed texts outside the window.
Play & Win

W

Waits until it is live
A rule for a version that is not yet live in that store is held back: the app is told about it only once that version is live. The Overview then adds “(waits until it is live in [store])”.
What a run photographs
The home screen first. Then, from App Studio, each ticked screen of the event's menu, opened from the app's side menu in menu order; when the screen list could not be read, the visible home tiles and the bottom-bar tabs instead. Last, when they exist: the first exhibitor's profile, its meeting-request screen and the first session in the schedule. The splash is photographed too but marked as not for the stores. An account type whose screens are all unticked gets the standard walk too.
Store screenshots
What App Studio has no section for
Push notifications, ExpoPlatform's internal tools, and the app's brand, bundle id or ownership.
Who sees it
Each block shows to Everyone, Guests only or Signed-in only.
Home screen · Custom pages

Y

Your apps
App Studio's first page: your organisation's apps in App Studio. Archived apps are not listed.
Your first app
With no app yet, Your apps says so and asks you to press “New app” to make your first one; the dialog then has no “Start from”.
Your old App Builder settings
App Studio starts once from the old App Builder's menu and settings. From then on, App Studio's copy is the one your App Studio app uses: later changes in the old App Builder do not reach it, and nothing saved here reaches the old apps.
Menu & content
Your page
A page written in App Studio. It belongs to one event, has 1 to 10 tabs and up to 60 blocks, and is listed with the badge “Your page”. An event holds at most 30.
Custom pages
Your screenshots (gallery)
Per store: the pictures of the newest successful capture, your own uploads, and any feature-video clips. A new successful capture replaces the previous capture's pictures; uploads stay until deleted. A captured picture that breaks a store rule carries a warning.
Store screenshots
Your wording
Your own text for one text of the app in one language. It is kept for this app only and for every event of the app (not per event), and replaces the standard text in the installed app.
