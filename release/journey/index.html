From test build to the stores

Your app reaches the App Store and Google Play in steps: ExpoPlatform sets it up in the stores, you build it and send it to your testers, you prepare the store listing and ask to publish, ExpoPlatform approves, the store reviews the release, and the release rolls out. This page follows a version along that way and links to the guide for each step.

Where App Studio shows the journey

Open Publish. Its Overview tab has a card for each store with six steps: Set up, Build, Test, Store listing, Publish and Live. Under each step is its state: Not yet, Needs attention, In progress, Waiting or Done. The card counts only the steps that are fully done (“[number] of 6 steps done”).

Below the steps, What now follows the first step that is not done. It names the next step and who takes it: Your move, Our team is on it or The store is on it. See How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores.

Before the first build: ExpoPlatform sets the app up

ExpoPlatform sets each new app up in the App Store and Google Play, under a store account it chooses. Until that is done, the app cannot be built: in Build & test both build buttons are greyed out, and the note above the list says “ExpoPlatform is setting this app up in the App Store and Google Play - you can build it once that is done”. Meanwhile Publish shows Not in the stores yet.

Test builds

In Build & test you build the app for iPhone and for Android. A finished build that is store-signed can go to your testers: the iPhone app through TestFlight, the Android app through Google Play internal testing. These are the two test channels App Studio offers, and sending to testers needs no approval from ExpoPlatform.

On TestFlight, Apple checks the build first; then your testers get an email invite. Google Play internal testing has no review. Each platform takes one release at a time, test and store releases alike: while one is still moving, another send for that platform is greyed out (“Another [iOS or Android] release is still in progress. Wait for it to finish.”).

The store listing

The store listing is your app's page in the stores: one listing for both stores, with texts, links and pictures in each language. You write it in Publish, on the Store listing tab. The pictures can come from Store studio in Store screenshots: Attach to the store listing puts your set on one listing language, replacing what each screenshot slot held. You can also Upload pictures on the listing itself.

Nothing you save there goes to the stores at once. App Studio has no button that sends the listing; the page says: “Your listing is sent to the stores together with your publication, once ExpoPlatform approves it.” The listing's badge shows where it stands:

Not sent yetThe listing has never been sent to the stores.
Changes not sentYou saved changes that have not reached the stores yet.
Sending to the storesThe listing is on its way to the stores.
In the storesThe stores have the current listing.
Sending failedThe last send to the stores failed.

Store reviewers sign in to your app before they approve it, so the listing's Store review block asks for a demo account: a login to a demo event. For iPhone, each store submission files this account in the App Store review details; without it, the iPhone submission fails and the release stays at Ready.

ExpoPlatform's approval

When you have a finished, signed build, you ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app: on the Releases tab, press Submit to the stores, tick the stores, choose a build for each and press Submit. The message “Sent to ExpoPlatform for approval” confirms it, and your request waits for ExpoPlatform to approve or decline it. One request can wait at a time.

Waiting for ExpoPlatform approval“Waiting for ExpoPlatform approval: [each store with its build], sent [when].” Your request waits for ExpoPlatform.
Approved“Approved [when]: [the builds] went to the stores' review. Follow it below.”
Declined“Declined: [ExpoPlatform's reason]” ExpoPlatform did not approve the request; the line gives their reason when they recorded one.
Approved, but the stores could not take it yet“Approved, but the stores could not take it yet. Our team is on it: [the builds].” ExpoPlatform approved, but publishing could not start.

When ExpoPlatform approves, your store listing is sent to the stores, and one store release is made for each build and sent on to that store's review.

Set by your ExpoPlatform team
Whether your app's publication waits for ExpoPlatform's approval is set by your ExpoPlatform team. It does by default. If they switch it off for your app, Submit sends the request on at once and the message reads “Sent to the stores”. What your ExpoPlatform team sets.

The store's review

Each store release takes one build to the store's review and then to everyone. Its card on Releases shows these steps:

DraftCreated, not yet uploaded.
UploadingThe build is being uploaded to the store.
ProcessingThe store is processing the build.
ReadyProcessed. It is sent for review on its own, unless Store review still has something to sort out.
SubmittedSent for the store's review.
In reviewThe store is reviewing it.
ApprovedApproved by the store; waiting for the rollout.
Rolling outReaching people step by step.
LiveEveryone can get this version.

A release can also stop on the way. Its card then says “This release stopped at [the status].” and, when the store gave a reason, shows the store's own message:

RejectedThe store turned it down.
HaltedThe rollout was stopped.
FailedIt could not be completed.
CancelledCancelled with Cancel release: App Studio stops following it. Anything the store already has stays there, and a review in progress carries on.
SupersededA newer release replaced it.

When a release does not go through, What now says “Your last release did not go through” and “See what happened and publish a new release.” A new store release goes through Submit to the stores again, and so through ExpoPlatform's approval. See How to follow a release through the store's review and How to cancel a store release.

The rollout

Once the store approves a release, the rollout brings it to people step by step. Two things go out by themselves: an app's first iPhone version, which Apple releases on approval, and the first 5% stage of an Android release. Later iPhone versions, and Android beyond 5%, wait for ExpoPlatform. Your ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout; ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

When the store has approved a release, Publish says “The rollout starts on its own and reaches people step by step, while the app stays healthy.” That holds only for an app's first iPhone version and for Android's first 5% stage.

While a rollout runs, health checks decide whether it moves forward, holds or stops: “The rollout moves on only while the app stays healthy.” The Rollout tab shows how far it has reached (People getting the new version), the planned curve, the health figures (Sessions without a crash, and App not responding on Android) and every check. The tab only shows the rollout; App Studio has no button to start, pause or stop it.

If the rollout is stopped, the release shows Halted and What now says “The rollout is stopped” and “ExpoPlatform is looking at it. You can also publish a fixed version.” See How to follow a rollout.

Your next version

A later version goes the same way: build, test, Submit to the stores, ExpoPlatform's approval, the store's review and the rollout. Not every change needs one. Most saved settings, such as colours, the home screen, the menu and pages, reach installed apps the next time the app refreshes, and saved texts in Translations within about 30 minutes. A new icon, name under the icon, splash picture or splash colour, and a home-screen widget switched on or off, reach phones only with the next build that is installed. See Update your app after launch and When your changes reach the app.
